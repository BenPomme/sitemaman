Therapy for expats in Barcelona: when life abroad starts to feel heavy

Expat therapy · Barcelona

Moving abroad can expand your life. It can also unsettle identity, relationships, work rhythms and the quiet sense of belonging that usually supports us without being noticed.

English / French / SpanishBarcelona and Sant Cugat30-minute first call free

Why expat life can make old questions louder

When you live away from your familiar language, family system and cultural codes, daily life often asks for more adaptation than people around you can see. Small things accumulate: explaining yourself, translating emotions, building new friendships, carrying professional pressure, parenting between cultures, or wondering who you are becoming.

Therapy offers a protected place to slow this down. The work is not about deciding whether you should stay or leave. It is about listening carefully to what your body, emotions and relationships are already showing.

What we may work on

  • anxiety, stress or persistent tension
  • loneliness, grief or loss of bearings
  • relationship patterns that repeat across contexts
  • burnout, workplace pressure or career transition
  • identity questions linked to language, culture and family roles

Some concrete situations

You followed a partner's job to Barcelona and left your own career behind. You manage a team in Spanish all day and have nothing left in the evening. Your children are growing up in Catalan and you sometimes feel like the outsider at your own dinner table. A parent is getting older back home, and every visit feels too short. You came for two years, it has been much longer, and you no longer know where home is.

In a mixed-language couple, one partner often lives in the other's language, and arguments about holidays or money can turn out to be about belonging, family loyalty or who gave up what. That work can also happen together, in couples therapy in Sant Cugat. If loneliness is what you feel most, my guide on loneliness when living abroad may be a useful first read.

How I work

I trained in Gestalt therapy at the Institut Gestalt in Barcelona, and my approach is humanistic and integrative, with Jungian influences. We work with what is happening now: what you feel as you speak, what tightens in your body, what repeats from one relationship to the next. I do not give advice or apply a ready-made method. My practice is supervised.

I also know expatriation from the inside. I have lived and worked in several countries, including in Asia, changed working language and rebuilt my professional path more than once. That does not make your story mine, but it helps me hear the parts that are hard to explain to people who never left. If working in English is your main criterion, see my page as an English-speaking therapist in Barcelona.

How the first step works

The first 30-minute conversation is free. It is a chance to describe what brings you here, ask practical questions, and sense whether the therapeutic frame feels right. Sessions can take place in Sant Cugat del Vallès, in the Barcelona area, or online.

Frequently asked questions

Is the first call a therapy session?

No. It is a free 30-minute conversation to get to know each other, clarify what brings you and go through practical questions. It is not a therapy session.

What language are sessions in?

English, French or Spanish. You can also move between languages during a session if that helps you say something more precisely.

Do you see clients from Barcelona city?

Yes. The practice is in Sant Cugat del Vallès, in the Barcelona area and reachable by public transport. Online sessions are an alternative if travelling does not fit your week.

Do I need a specific problem to start?

No. A sense that life abroad has become heavy is enough. The first call helps put words to what you are looking for.
